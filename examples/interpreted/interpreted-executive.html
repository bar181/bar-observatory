Reading the evidence

What the numbers mean

Reading the headline figures together, not in isolation.

100% task completion across two weeks of real work is a strong signal on its own, but it's worth reading alongside the test evidence rather than in isolation: 373 test-result lines were captured over the window, 344 passing (92%), and the window's last captured test run was green. 29 failures inside that count aren't a red flag by themselves — that's what iterative, test-driven work looks like mid-stream — the number that would actually matter is whether the final state was clean, and it was.

The 60 error results (1.9% of 3,171 tool actions) merit one clarifying note: a meaningful share of these are the safety system doing its job — commands like rm -rf and credential-scanning greps being blocked before they ran, not things going wrong. The engineering-audience commentary breaks this down precisely; the short version for you is that the "60 errors" headline number overstates real risk on its own.

The 47 real commits against 68 human prompts is roughly 2 commits for every 3 prompts — a healthy cadence for iterative, reviewed work, not a sign of either stalling (too few commits) or unreviewed rubber-stamping (too many).
